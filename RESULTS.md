# popsim: results so far (2026-10-20)

Every Twin-2K-500 participant's wave-4 answers, predicted by digital twins and scored against what the same people
actually answered. Our twin is [autotrust/JEV-9B](https://huggingface.co/autotrust/JEV-9B), self-hosted with vLLM on
Modal. For each question it returns a distribution over the options. **J** takes the most likely option, as a
point-predicting twin does; **P** draws one option from the distribution. The comparison twins are the 13 simulation
runs the Twin authors published with the dataset: GPT-4.1-mini, GPT-4.1 and Gemini 2.5 Flash, with various personas.

Every table below is generated from the files in `results/`; section 7 lists the script behind each one.

## Headline findings after the release review

1. **A separate-sample majority baseline still exceeds the tested twins on answer similarity.**
   It learns from 300 humans and is evaluated on 1,758 different humans (section 0). It has
   same-question human training labels, so this does not establish performance on unseen items.
2. **Cross-person correlation does not establish unique individual knowledge.** A demographic
   ridge baseline reaches about 0.104, comparable to several twins at about 0.10. Demographic
   patterns can explain association across people; no incremental individual signal is established here.
3. **Calibration remains useful within this panel after removing target statistics from fitting.**
   Pricing improvements are unchanged. The corrected polling and A/B results are in section 4.
   The survey is additional calibration data; rich profiles for all 2,058 people already exist.
4. **Raw twin levels and effect sizes remain unreliable.** Product ranking and segment directions
   are more promising than absolute shares. These observations apply to the tested model/prompt
   combinations on this dataset, not to all synthetic respondents.

Compact, attributed snapshots and provenance are in [artifacts/](artifacts/). The baseline,
correlation and calibration tables below are regenerated by `python -m popsim.report`.

## Definitions

- **Accuracy.** The Twin authors' scorer, run unmodified. Per question it is 1 − |prediction − answer| / range,
  scored against the person's wave 1-3 answer. It is averaged per task, then over 16 tasks; anchoring is skipped
  for every arm. This is normalized absolute-error similarity, not exact-match accuracy.
- **vs retest.** Accuracy divided by the humans' own retest accuracy (their wave-4 answers scored the same way).
- **Person signal.** For each question, the correlation across people between an arm's answer and the person's own
  wave 1-3 answer, averaged over 78 questions. A constant answer counts as 0. Demographic group
  differences can contribute to this association; it does not isolate unique individual knowledge.
  - The 40 pricing questions are left out: each person saw a different product at a different price there, so
    knowing the offer alone scores about 0.4.
  - The 95% intervals bootstrap individual questions, not participants or task families; they are
    descriptive within-dataset intervals, and dependent items can make them optimistic.
- **Shares.** On the 43 multi-option items:
  - the median Spearman between the arm's answer shares and the humans' wave-4 shares;
  - the mean RMSE in percentage points;
  - **spread**, the arm's answer SD divided by the humans'.
- **Target-population oracle diagnostics (historical tables).** Both use every other participant's
  wave-4 answer to the same version of the question. The **majority answer** picks the most common
  answer; the **population draw** samples those shares. They have access to other test participants'
  labels and are not deployable baselines for an unsurveyed question. Section 0 uses separate samples.

**Context parity.** Our "summary" and "full" personas are the dataset's own `persona_summary` and
`wave1_3_persona_text`, which the published runs are named after. Those runs released outputs, not prompts, so this
can't be checked byte for byte. The structured wave-split persona and target have no overlapping
question IDs across all 2,058 participants. The summaries are supplied by the dataset authors;
keyword checks are not a complete audit of their generation or paraphrased answer leakage.

The formats differ. The published twins got the whole questionnaire (~7k tokens) in one prompt at temperature 0
and wrote all their answers at once. JEV-9B gets one question at a time, and its distribution is read from the
model's log-probabilities. Sliders and 0-20 entries are binned to 11 options, which costs at most 0.006 accuracy.

## 0. Baselines trained on separate humans

Seed 0 selects 300 training participants and 1,758 disjoint evaluation participants. All arms
below are scored on those same evaluation participants and the same 16 tasks / 78 correlation
questions. Majority/draw use only training answers (wording matched when available). Their
fallback pools the same question ID when exact wording is unseen, including pricing positions.

The demographic baseline is ridge regression with fixed lambda=10 and categorical age, gender,
race, education, income, party and ideology. It learns each question from the 300-person training
sample, rounds/clips to valid answer levels, and is not tuned on the test sample. It does not
model pricing offers. These are same-item, different-person comparisons; the baselines and twins
have different information budgets.

## 4. Survey a few, simulate the rest — corrected calibration

A client already has rich profiles for the 2,058-person panel, surveys 300 participants on some
units and uses twins on all units. The experiment measures the value of these **additional**
calibration responses, not the total human-data cost of building the panel.

The fit uses an intercept per price level or segment plus a single scale on the twin prediction.
A/B uses a scale without an intercept. Polling gaps and A/B mean differences are divided by the
fixed questionnaire range during fitting. No full-panel or held-out human variance enters the
predictor. Human SDs are used only to express prediction errors after fitting. This replaces the
previous target-SD-normalized fits, so earlier polling/A/B calibration figures are superseded.

- Errors: pricing RMSE in percentage points; polling RMSE in item SDs; A/B RMSE in human pooled SDs.
- The baseline predicts the survey's mean per price level/segment, or zero A/B effect.
- There are 500 seeded splits of surveyed units and calibration participants per setting.
- Evaluation covers unsurveyed units across the full panel, including the calibration participants;
  this tests transfer to new units within a panel, not to a new population.
- The ensemble averages seven published runs, including several related GPT-4.1-mini variants.
- A/B splits hold out contrasts, not entire experiment families; some contrasts share controls.
- "Beats" is an empirical fraction of splits, not a p-value or independent replication rate.

## 5. Cost and the decision cache

| run | GPU | time | metered cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| smoke test + three 200-person pilots | 1x H100 | | $3.24 |
| summary persona, all 2,058 (174,696 decisions, 574M prompt tokens, 89% from the prefix cache) | 1x H100 | 50 min | $3.62 |
| everything else (published runs, scoring, industry metrics, calibration) | local CPU | | $0 |

Modal credits covered all of it. Every decision is saved in `cache/decisions.sqlite` (231,000 decisions, 175 MB).
Each is stored under a hash of model, revision and exact prompt, with the persona and question it came from.
- The 174,696 decisions from the full run also keep the raw log-probabilities and token counts.
- The 56,304 pilot decisions were imported from their result files, so they have probabilities only.
- A run only sends what the cache lacks and saves each batch of 25 people as it returns.

## 6. Caveats and what hasn't been tested

- **One dataset, one seed.** The intervals cover question sampling, not other surveys or populations.
  No benefit beyond demographic prediction is established by the current correlation metric.
- **Information budgets differ.** Baselines receive same-item human labels from other people;
  twins receive rich profiles. Calibration assumes those profiles already exist.
- **JEV-9B and the published twins differ in format as well as model.** See "Context parity" above. The tie on
  person signal is a tie of model plus format.
- **No current frontier model has been run.** The newest models compared are GPT-4.1 and Gemini 2.5 Flash, from the
  published runs. Closed models need an API key, which is outside this project's Modal-only setup.
- **Larger open models, untested.** These are public and could run on Modal (rough estimates; a 200-person pilot
  would measure the real cost):

  | model | what it would test | pilot (200) | all 2,058 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Qwen3.5-9B (JEV-9B's base, without JEV's decision training) | whether that training matters | ~$0.5 | ~$4 |
  | JEV-27B | whether a bigger JEV moves the ceiling | ~$1.5 | ~$12 |
  | Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, FP8 (406 GB, 8 GPUs) | whether a frontier-class open model moves the ceiling | ~$8–10 | ~$30–50 |

  General models need a second decider path, asking for an option letter with thinking off, and a cache key per
  model. Neither exists yet.
- **Anchoring is skipped** for every arm. Its answers are open-ended numbers scored against deciles of the human
  answers.
- **The "random answers" baseline is unseeded,** so it moves by about 0.005 between scorings.

Logs of the latest runs are in `results/score-*.log`, `results/uses.log` and `results/calibrate.log`. Billing
snapshots are `results/billing-*.json`. `results/` and `cache/` are git-ignored. Compact aggregate snapshots are included under `artifacts/`.
